# Multi-Step Content Engine

## What it does

Takes a topic, target audience, and content goal and returns a complete, production-ready content package. One input session replaces what would normally be a 2-3 hour content planning meeting. Everything produced is interconnected — the pillar article, emails, social angles, lead magnet, and calendar all reinforce each other around a single strategic throughline.

## Why it's better than a plain LLM prompt

Generic content prompts return generic outputs in isolation. This skill enforces a strategic brief step first, which grounds every subsequent output in a clear audience pain point and outcome goal. The six-section structure ensures nothing is skipped — most planning sessions miss either the lead magnet, the calendar, or the email layer. All three are produced here automatically.

## Inputs

Provide the following three things:
- **Topic** — the subject or theme of the content push (e.g., "productivity systems for freelancers")
- **Target audience** — who you are creating for (e.g., "freelance designers earning $50-100k/year")
- **Content goal** — what you want the audience to do or believe (e.g., "book a discovery call" or "opt in to the email list")

## Outputs

1. **Strategic Brief** — 3-4 sentence foundation for the content plan
2. **Pillar Article Outline** — full H1/H2/H3 outline with section descriptions
3. **Email Subject Lines** — 3 options with psychological trigger notes
4. **Social Post Angles** — 5 distinct frames for platform content
5. **Lead Magnet Concept** — title, format, contents, and opt-in hook
6. **4-Week Content Calendar** — week-by-week plan with content, social, email, and audience goal per week

## Permissions used

`memory:write` — saves a compact summary of the content plan to memory for future reference.

## Memory behavior

Writes a structured summary of the topic, audience, and calendar overview to memory after generating the plan. This allows future sessions to reference prior content plans and avoid topic overlap.

## Ideal use cases

- Launching a new product or service and needing a full content runway
- Planning a monthly content theme from scratch
- A solo founder or small team needing to punch above their weight on content
- Content agencies building rapid first-draft plans for clients
